01 / ENGINEERING & METHODOLOGY

From cooling load to electricity savings.

The alternatives provide the same assumed cooling service. Equipment performance and control assumptions change the electricity needed to deliver it.

How I built the engineering case

  1. 01

    Establish the baseline

    Two assumed 600-ton water-cooled chillers, with a shared load profile and separate auxiliary schedules.

  2. 02

    Model equipment electricity

    Four chiller load bins plus fan, pump and cooling-tower power estimates.

  3. 03

    Link the operating costs

    Apply an illustrative $0.20/kWh tariff, maintenance reductions and recurring analytics costs.

Engineering insight

Fans, pumps and tower fans deliver 75.5% of C’s modeled electricity savings. Chiller efficiency alone cannot validate the proposal.

Evidence priorities

  • Baseline capital: removing A’s two future capital events and their depreciation takes C’s base NPV to −$950,575 in the report’s severe diagnostic.
  • Owner benefit: retaining only 50% of net operating savings takes C’s NPV to −$426,932.
  • Delivered savings: 70% energy realization gives C NPV of −$80,421; separately, 60% maintenance realization gives −$160,465.

Assumptions Validation, pp. 8–9. Each diagnostic holds other inputs fixed. These are not proposed changes to the baseline.

What the model does not establish

  • No survey, equipment measurements, contractor bids or owner tax determination validate the inputs.
  • The ten-year horizon excludes terminal service value and later tax benefits, especially relevant to B. Financing, incentives and avoided carbon penalties are excluded.
  • Tax is illustrative: 28%, immediate deduction use, 39-year real property and five-year computer MACRS; no bonus or Section 179 deduction is assumed.
  • The LL97 screen leaves all alternatives above the modeled 2030 limit, omits 2035–2036, and is not a compliance determination. Gas consumption is unchanged.
Validated calculations ≠ verified performance.

The supplied Step 3 report documents 94 independent comparisons. Step 2 records control corrections to the original model. Those are historical project checks; they do not establish site savings or investment readiness.

04 / INVESTMENT RECOMMENDATION

Advance C for further evaluation.
Verify the case before committing capital.

Partial upgrade offers the strongest base-case NPV and uses less capital. Its small surplus and late discounted recovery leave limited room for error.

Why C merits further study

  • ✓

    Better ten-year value. B’s additional $3.331m is not justified by the modeled incremental benefits.

  • ✓

    A modest return, not a robust approval case. C’s 9.44% XIRR barely exceeds the 8.5% discount rate; discounted payback is 9.12 years.

Before funding

  1. Confirm equipment condition and future repair needs.
  2. Obtain installed bids and measure operating performance.
  3. Verify utility savings, lease allocation and tax treatment.

This independent hypothetical study supports further investigation. It does not approve either upgrade or establish lifetime performance beyond the ten-year planning window.
